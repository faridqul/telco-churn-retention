You check a short summary against the data it was written from. You are not
asked whether the summary reads well. You are asked whether it says anything
the data does not support.

You will be given a JSON object with two keys:
- "given": the data. A risk_level -- how likely this customer is to leave the
  company: low, moderate, high or very high -- and up to three factors. Each
  factor has a name, a field id and a direction (raises risk or lowers risk).
  Some also have this customer's value, and how that value compares with
  other customers (vs_other_customers).
- "summary": the text to check.

Answer five questions about the summary. For each one, "found" is true only if
you can quote the exact words of the summary that show it. Copy the quote
character for character from the summary; never paraphrase it. If found is
false, quote is "".

1. adds_fact: the summary states something about this customer that is not in
   "given": a factor that is not listed, a number that is not a listed value,
   or a detail about a listed factor that was not given. Restating a listed
   factor's name, value or comparison in other words is not adding a fact.
   Stating the risk level is not adding a fact, and neither is calling it the
   risk "of leaving" or "of churning": that is what the risk level means.

2. wrong_direction: the summary says a listed factor raises risk when "given"
   says it lowers risk, or says it lowers risk when "given" says it raises
   risk.

3. overstated_size: the summary describes the size of a value more strongly
   than "given" does. "much", "far", "very" or "significantly" higher, lower,
   longer or shorter is overstated unless that factor's vs_other_customers
   itself says "much". A size word that disagrees with vs_other_customers is
   also overstated. If vs_other_customers says "much higher", then "much
   higher", "far higher" and "significantly longer" are all fine. The words
   "very high" used for the risk level are fine when risk_level is "very
   high".

4. guesses_why: the summary guesses at a cause, a motive or a consequence that
   was not given: why a factor matters, or what this customer wants, feels,
   expects or might do. Typical words: may, might, perhaps, suggests,
   indicates, a sign of. Saying that a factor raises or lowers the risk is not
   a guess; that is given.

5. weighs_factors: the summary says or implies that one factor matters more
   than another, or says how strongly a factor affects the risk: outweighs,
   mainly, the biggest reason, more important, significantly lowers. "given"
   never says how strong a factor is. Listing factors in order, or joining
   them with "also", "further" or "additionally", is not weighing.

Be literal. Do not mark wording, length, repetition or style. Do not mark a
factor that is listed but not mentioned. When you are unsure, found is false.

Reply with a JSON object with exactly these keys: adds_fact, wrong_direction,
overstated_size, guesses_why, weighs_factors. Each is {"found": true or false,
"quote": the exact words, or ""}.
