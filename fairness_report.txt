=== 1. RELIABILITY GATE ======================================================

  A recall estimate's precision is set by the number of CHURNERS in the
  subgroup, not its total size. Bands applied throughout:

    >= 150  churners   solid        ~+/-7pp on recall
    >= 100  churners   usable       ~+/-9pp
    >= 50   churners   marginal     ~+/-13pp, companion required
    >= 30   churners   marginal-    wide, treat with suspicion
    <  30   churners   INSUFFICIENT -- no rate is reported at all

=== 5. PER-GROUP THRESHOLDS -- THE COST OF RECALL PARITY =====================

  Economics: CLV $200, contact cost $20, success rate 30%
  Test set: 229 seniors (89 churners), 1176 non-seniors (283 churners)

  policy                              thr sen  thr non     profit  recall gap
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  shipped: one threshold                 0.40     0.40     6,660       23.3pp
  (a) per-group profit optimum           0.40     0.36     6,680       18.3pp
  (b) per-group + recall parity          0.56     0.36     6,460       -0.8pp

  COST OF RECALL PARITY  =  (a) - (b)  =  $220  (3.3% of (a))
  Both groups end up catching 67.4% of their real churners.

  These are TEST-SET dollars on 1,405 customers, not an annual figure.
  (a) is a diagnostic upper bound, NOT a deployment proposal: setting
  different cutoffs by a protected attribute is disparate treatment and
  is legally fraught in most jurisdictions. The number that matters is
  the GAP between (a) and (b) -- the price of the constraint.

=== 6. WHY THERE IS NO 'CORRECT' ANSWER HERE =================================

  When two groups have different base rates -- and in this test set
  seniors churn at 38.9% against 24.1% for everyone else --
  recall parity, precision parity and flag-rate parity CANNOT all hold
  at once. This is a
  theorem, not a limitation of this model (Chouldechova 2017;
  Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan 2016). Any threshold satisfying
  one criterion necessarily violates the others.

  This report therefore chooses RECALL PARITY (equal opportunity) and
  says so, rather than presenting a computed 'fair' answer. The reason:
  a retention offer is an allocated BENEFIT, so the harm being measured
  is a real churner never being contacted. Precision parity would
  instead equalise wasted spend, which is the company's interest rather
  than the customer's.

  A reader who disagrees with that choice should read section 5's
  numbers and substitute their own criterion. The numbers are reported
  so the trade-off is visible, not to settle it.

=== 7. SUMMARY IN PLAIN LANGUAGE =============================================

  Groups with a real gap (bigger than 10 points, and the confidence
  interval does not include zero):
    - seniorcitizen: recall is 23 points HIGHER for 'senior' than 'non-senior'
    - partner: recall is 19 points HIGHER for 'no partner' than 'has partner'
    - dependents: recall is 19 points HIGHER for 'no dependents' than 'has dependents'
    - family_tie: recall is 16 points HIGHER for 'alone' than 'has family'
    - is_auto_pay: recall is 30 points HIGHER for 'manual pay' than 'auto-pay'

  Thin but reportable: dependents/has dependents, seniorcitizen/senior.
  Read the intervals, not the point estimates.

  Which way do these run? In every case the gap favours the group
  that churns MORE. Seniors, people without a partner, people
  without dependents and people who pay manually are all MORE likely
  to be contacted when they are about to leave, not less. That is the
  expected result of one threshold over groups with different base
  rates -- the model ranks higher-risk groups higher, so more of them
  clear the cutoff. The people being missed are the LOW-risk groups:
  a churning customer with a partner or dependents is meaningfully
  less likely to be offered retention than a churning customer alone.

  What fairness costs: making the model catch the same share of
  churning seniors as churning non-seniors would cost $220
  of the $6,680 that separate per-group cutoffs
  could earn -- about 3% -- on this 1,405-customer test set.

  But note HOW parity is reached: by raising the senior cutoff, so
  FEWER at-risk seniors get contacted, not by contacting more
  non-seniors. Equal recall here is levelling down. That is an
  argument against enforcing it, and it is visible only because the
  per-group thresholds are printed rather than summarised.

  What was decided: nothing changes. The model keeps one threshold of
  0.4 for everyone. Using a different cutoff for seniors would
  mean treating people differently because of their age, which is the
  thing fairness work is supposed to prevent -- so the per-group
  numbers above are a measurement, not a proposal. They exist so that
  if someone asks 'is this model fair to older customers?', the answer
  is a number and a stated trade-off rather than a shrug.
