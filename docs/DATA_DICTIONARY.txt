=== 1. DATASET FACTS ===========================================================

  Rows removed      22 exact duplicates, dropped AFTER customerid so the
                    comparison is on real attributes, not a unique key
  Churn             1,857 Yes / 5,164 No = 26.45%. This is the PR-AUC
                    baseline -- always quote PR-AUC against 0.2645, not 0.5

=== 2. IDENTIFIER AND TARGET ===================================================

customerid                                                      DROPPED
  String, e.g. "7590-VHVEG", unique per row. Dropped in cell 3, before
  deduplication -- with it present no two rows could ever look identical.

churn                                                            TARGET
  "Yes"/"No" -> 1/0. 26.45% positive. Predicting "No" for everyone scores
  73.55% accuracy, which is why accuracy is not the headline metric.


=== 3. RAW FEATURES (19) -- exactly what /predict accepts ======================

Allowed values are enforced in two places that tests assert must agree:
api.Customer's Literal types, and config.CATEGORICAL_DOMAINS.

--- DEMOGRAPHICS --------------------------------------------------------------

gender                 "Male" | "Female"
  50.4% / 49.6%. Churn 26.1% / 26.8% -- 1.03x, essentially no signal. Useful
  as a fairness control: near-equal base rates mean parity criteria are
  mutually compatible, so any disparity found is a real model artifact.

seniorcitizen          0 | 1   (integer, not "Yes"/"No")
  83.7% / 16.3%. Churn 23.5% / 41.6% -- 1.77x. The only categorical stored
  numerically in the raw file. Validated ge=0, le=1, so it sits in
  config.NUMERIC_COLUMNS despite behaving as a category.

partner                "Yes" | "No"
  48.5% / 51.5%. Churn 19.7% / 32.8% -- 1.66x.

dependents             "Yes" | "No"
  30.1% / 69.9%. Churn 15.5% / 31.2% -- 2.01x. Customers with dependents are
  markedly stickier.

--- PHONE ---------------------------------------------------------------------

phoneservice           "Yes" | "No"
  90.3% / 9.7%. Churn 26.6% / 24.9% -- 1.07x, almost no signal alone.

multiplelines          "Yes" | "No" | "No phone service"
  42.3% / 48.0% / 9.7%. Churn 28.6% / 24.9% / 24.9%.
  TRAP: "No phone service" is exactly the 682 rows where phoneservice=="No".
  Fully determined by another column -- a structural placeholder, not a state.

--- INTERNET ------------------------------------------------------------------

internetservice        "DSL" | "Fiber optic" | "No"
  34.5% / 44.0% / 21.5%. Churn 18.9% / 41.8% / 7.2% -- 5.8x.
  One of the strongest predictors. Fiber churns at 5.8x the no-internet rate;
  plausibly a price/expectation effect, since fiber is the premium tier.

The six add-ons share one domain -- "Yes" | "No" | "No internet service" --
and one placeholder: the 1,512 rows where internetservice=="No" carry
"No internet service" in all six columns simultaneously.

  ADD-ON              % Yes    churn No    churn Yes    spread (Yes vs No)
  ------------------- -------- ---------- ------------ ------------------
  onlinesecurity       28.8%     41.6%       14.6%          2.85x
  techsupport          29.1%     41.5%       15.2%          2.74x
  onlinebackup         34.6%     39.8%       21.5%          1.85x
  deviceprotection     34.5%     39.0%       22.5%          1.73x
  streamingmovies      38.9%     33.5%       29.9%          1.12x
  streamingtv          38.6%     33.3%       30.1%          1.11x

  The four PROTECTIVE add-ons cut churn by 1.7x to 2.9x. The two STREAMING
  add-ons move it by 1.1x -- essentially nothing. Streaming is entertainment;
  the others are lock-in and support. The columns are identically shaped, so
  this split is easy to miss, and it is part of what total_services captures.

--- ACCOUNT AND BILLING -------------------------------------------------------

contract               "Month-to-month" | "One year" | "Two year"
  54.9% / 21.0% / 24.1%. Churn 42.6% / 11.3% / 2.8% -- 15.2x.
  THE strongest single predictor. Largely mechanical -- a two-year customer
  cannot leave without breaking the contract -- but no less useful for it.

paperlessbilling       "Yes" | "No"
  59.3% / 40.7%. Churn 33.4% / 16.3% -- 2.05x. Likely a proxy for digital
  engagement and newer customers rather than a cause.

paymentmethod          "Electronic check" | "Mailed check"
                     | "Bank transfer (automatic)" | "Credit card (automatic)"
  33.6% / 22.7% / 22.0% / 21.7%.
  Churn 45.1% / 18.9% / 16.7% / 15.2% -- 3.0x.
  TRAP: "Electronic check" is MANUAL -- the customer initiates each payment.
  It sounds automatic, is not, and is the highest-churn category in the whole
  dataset. Only the two labelled "(automatic)" are automatic, which is exactly
  what is_auto_pay encodes. Automatic-vs-manual is also a reasonable proxy for
  banking access, making it the most defensible socioeconomic axis available.

--- NUMERIC -------------------------------------------------------------------

tenure                 integer, months with the company
  0 to 72. Median 29, mean 32.5. Validated ge=0, no upper bound: the tree
  ensemble saturates, so tenure=10**15 scores the same as tenure=1000.
  11 customers are at 0 (new, not yet billed). Strongly protective --
  0-6 months churns at 52.9%, 49+ months at 9.5%.

monthlycharges         float, currency
  18.25 to 118.75. Median 70.40, mean 64.85. Validated ge=0 AND
  allow_inf_nan=False. Infinity must be blocked explicitly because inf >= 0
  is True, and it would then reach the scaler and raise mid-request (a 500
  instead of a 422). NaN is worse: SimpleImputer would silently replace it
  with the training median, turning garbage into a confident prediction.

totalcharges           float, currency -- THE ONLY OPTIONAL FIELD
  18.80 to 8,684.80. Median 1,403.88, mean 2,290.35. Validated ge=0,
  allow_inf_nan=False, nullable. Blank in 11 raw rows, all tenure==0, which
  is why the raw column parses as string and needs pd.to_numeric. The
  pipeline's SimpleImputer exists for exactly these rows, and
  tests/test_integration.py forces 3 fixture rows to tenure=0 so the case is
  exercised for every seed rather than left to chance.


=== 4. ENGINEERED FEATURES (6) =================================================

From feature_engineering_telco.py :: engineer_features(), imported by the
notebook, api.py, telco_model.py, config.py and two test files -- the single
most load-bearing module in the repo. config.validate_feature_schema() crashes
at startup if these do not yield exactly the 25 columns in
model_metadata.json["feature_columns"].

total_services         count of "Yes" across the six add-ons, 0-6
  0: n=2,197 churn 21.1%      4: n=  852 churn 22.3%
  1: n=  966 churn 45.8%      5: n=  571 churn 12.4%
  2: n=1,033 churn 35.8%      6: n=  284 churn  5.3%
  3: n=1,118 churn 27.4%
  NOT MONOTONIC. Churn RISES from 0 to 1 service, then falls steadily. The 0
  bucket is contaminated: 1,512 of its 2,197 rows are customers with no
  internet at all, who churn at 7.2%. Remove them and the relationship is
  monotonic decreasing as expected. Do not read the 0 bucket as "few services
  is safe" -- read it as "mostly phone-only customers".

is_auto_pay            1 if paymentmethod contains "automatic", else 0
  43.7% / 56.3%. Churn 16.0% / 34.6% -- 2.16x. Collapses the 4-way
  paymentmethod into the distinction that actually matters.
  The implementation calls .astype('string') before .str because an entirely
  blank paymentmethod column reads as float64 from read_csv and the .str
  accessor would raise an error naming pandas internals rather than the real
  problem. It degrades to is_auto_pay=0 instead. (AUDIT.md M9)

family_tie             1 if partner=="Yes" OR dependents=="Yes", else 0
  53.6% / 46.4%. Churn 19.8% / 34.1% -- 1.72x. Combines two weaker signals
  into a stronger one, and gives better statistical power for subgroup
  analysis than either parent column alone.

contractvstenure       contract mapped {Mth:1, 1yr:2, 2yr:3} * tenure
  0 to 216. Median 37, mean 68.7. An interaction term. A tree can recover
  this via two sequential splits, so it closes no capability gap -- it earns
  its place because of XGBoost's per-tree column subsampling: if a tree drops
  contract or tenure, this column carries the interaction anyway.

average_monthly_charges   totalcharges/tenure if tenure>0, else monthlycharges
  13.775 to 121.40. Median 70.45, mean 64.85. What the customer has
  historically paid per month. The fallback covers the 11 tenure==0 rows and
  prevents a division by zero.

charge_change_ratio    monthlycharges/average_monthly_charges if >0, else 1.0
  0.636 to 1.451. Median exactly 1.000, mean 1.002, with 603 rows at exactly
  1.0. >1 means paying more now than historically (a recent rise or upgrade).
  CAUTION: very narrow range. For most customers totalcharges/tenure is close
  to monthlycharges by construction, so this carries less information than its
  name suggests. Treat it as a weak feature.


=== 5. TRAPS ===================================================================

5.1  "No internet service" is not a third state. Same 1,512 rows, all six
     add-on columns, exactly where internetservice=="No". The encoder
     therefore produces six perfectly correlated indicators -- harmless for a
     tree ensemble, would matter for a linear model. Same story for
     "No phone service" (682 rows, multiplelines).

5.2  The 0-services bucket is contaminated (see total_services), and
     "Electronic check" is a manual method (see paymentmethod). These two
     are the most likely things to be misread in this dataset.

5.3  Unknown categories fail SILENTLY. The pipeline's OneHotEncoder uses
     handle_unknown='ignore', so an unrecognised category becomes an
     all-zeros block indistinguishable from "no information" -- no error,
     just a different answer. A casing slip alone ('month-to-month' for
     'Month-to-month') moves the reference customer from 0.5700 to 0.1017 and
     flips the retention decision. This is why config.CATEGORICAL_DOMAINS and
     the API's Literal types exist and are asserted to agree.

5.4  Out-of-range NUMBERS are safe; out-of-range CATEGORIES are not. The tree
     ensemble saturates, so there is deliberately no range check on numerics.
     The hazard is entirely on the categorical side.

Preprocessing inside the pipeline (cell 12, make_preprocessor()):
  numeric      -> SimpleImputer -> StandardScaler
  categorical  -> OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore')

Raw source columns are KEPT alongside their derived versions -- partner and
dependents survive next to family_tie, paymentmethod next to is_auto_pay. The
engineered columns are additions, not replacements.

Enforced in:
  feature_engineering_telco.py      the 6 formulas, defined once
  config.CATEGORICAL_DOMAINS        allowed values, all 15 categoricals
  config.NUMERIC_COLUMNS            seniorcitizen, tenure, monthlycharges,

  config.validate_input_frame()     the batch path's equivalent of Pydantic
  tests/test_input_validation.py    asserts the API and config domains agree

If engineer_features() changes MEANING without changing column names, bump
config.SUPPORTED_FEATURE_SCHEMA_VERSION and the notebook's stamped
feature_schema_version together -- the column check cannot see that case.
